The December messages.

The $25 January credit goes to every client. How we say it changes depending on whether we've seen them recently, so there are two sets of messages here. Nothing sends until you've approved the copy and we've checked the consent wording.

The offer, as agreed

What everyone gets

Book an in-office service in December, get $25 toward a service in January.

The January service needs to be $75 or more. In-office services only, so no products or gift cards. The credit is applied at checkout by us, so there is nothing for anyone to keep hold of or mention at the desk.

One thing still to pin down

Does the $75 minimum apply to the January service, the December one, or both? Written as it stands it reads as the January booking, which is the usual way round. Worth confirming before it goes out.

Two audiences

Same offer, different opening. Someone who came in last month shouldn't be told we haven't seen them in a while.

Clients who've been in recently

Shorter, and only two messages. These people are already coming, so the job is just making sure they know about the credit. Over-messaging an active client is how you lose one.

Clients we haven't seen in a while

This group gets the credit too, but the message does a second job: finding out why they stopped coming. Two versions to choose between.

Read the sequence as

The difference between them

The check-in

Opens like a note from you, not a promotion. It says we've noticed they've been away, invites them to tell you if something went wrong last time, and only then mentions the offer.

The invitation to complain is deliberate. On a list of people who drifted away, a few honest replies about why are worth more than the bookings, and it makes the message read as a person rather than a campaign.

The calendar

Opens on December filling up. Shorter, more direct, and it gives a practical reason to act now rather than a personal one.

It suits the part of the list that doesn't respond to being checked in on but does respond to a deadline. One thing to confirm first, in the orange box below the email.

One thing for you before it sends

We need to know what your current forms already say about texting. Have a look at your intake and consent templates in PracticeQ for any wording about text messages. You'll find one of three things:

What you're looking for

Reminders only, something like "appointment reminders via text." Most common, and it doesn't stretch to cover a message like these.

Wording that mentions offers or promotions. Anyone who signed that is covered.

Nothing at all. Worth fixing regardless, separately from this.

Whatever you find, we'd also add a separate tick box to new client intake going forward. You can answer below, or just tell us you're not sure and we'll look together.

Heads up on opt-outs

PracticeQ treats STOP as all-or-nothing. If someone replies STOP to one of these, it also switches off their appointment reminders. That's why the last text promises there's no fourth one, and why we'd tell people about it upfront on the new tick box.
